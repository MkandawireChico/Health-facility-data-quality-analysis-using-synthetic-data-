# Illustrative Resource Capacity Planning Methodology

## Purpose

The resource-planning layer demonstrates how a health-facility analytics platform can compare capacity gaps under a constrained pool of abstract resources. It is a scenario-analysis exercise for a synthetic portfolio, not a staffing plan or budget recommendation.

## Inputs and assumptions

- Facility identity, district label, facility type, urban/rural classification and catchment population come from the generated synthetic facility dimension.
- Illustrative requirement = ceiling(catchment population / 10,000 × configurable units-per-10,000) + facility-type increment.
- Default increments are 1 abstract unit for a health centre, 2 for a community hospital and 4 for a district hospital.
- Synthetic available capacity is generated by multiplying the illustrative requirement by a seeded uniform factor from 0.55 to 1.15 and rounding down.
- The default scenario pool is 120 abstract units. It can be changed in the CLI or Streamlit scenario input.

These parameters are deliberately visible and editable. They are not derived from official Malawi staffing establishments, national norms, facility registers or validated service workload standards.

## Allocation strategies

Three deterministic heuristics allocate one unit at a time and never exceed a facility's calculated baseline gap:

1. **Gap priority:** assign the next unit to the facility with the largest remaining gap; Facility_ID breaks ties.
2. **Rural-weighted equity:** assign using a 1.25 multiplier for rural facilities and 1.0 otherwise; Facility_ID breaks ties. This is an illustrative equity weight, not an adopted policy.
3. **Equal-share rounds:** prioritise facilities with the fewest allocations so far, with stable Facility_ID tie-breaking.

The pool may remain partly unallocated when all modelled gaps are filled. Scenario summaries compare total allocation, residual gaps, facilities fully covered and mean post-allocation capacity coverage.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Resource units are abstract units, not people, staff headcounts, equipment quantities or currency.
- Catchment population is a planning proxy only. It is not observed patient volume, unique people served, population coverage or service utilisation.
- Facility capacity and requirements are synthetic; they must not be presented as actual Malawi facility constraints.
- Allocation outputs are deterministic heuristics, not mathematically validated optima or operational recommendations.
- A real deployment would require approved staffing/resource norms, verified facility-level inventories, workload and service-mix evidence, budget constraints, local governance and domain review.

## Outputs

The pipeline writes:
- `facility_resource_capacity.csv`
- `resource_allocation_scenarios.csv`
- `resource_allocation_scenario_summary.csv`
- `resource_planning_report.json`

Run independently with `python src/resource_planning.py --resource-pool 120`, or as part of `python src/build_platform.py`.
